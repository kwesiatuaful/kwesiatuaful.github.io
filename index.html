About

Research at the intersection of policy and development.

My research lies at the intersection of economic policy, development economics, public policy, and program evaluation. I examine how policies, institutions, and natural-resource governance affect economic development and human welfare.

I am particularly interested in questions where policy participation is non-random and where effects may differ across countries, institutions, and time. My empirical work combines causal inference, applied econometrics, and policy evaluation to study whether interventions achieve their intended outcomes and the mechanisms through which they operate.

My broader interests include economic development, natural-resource governance, institutions, poverty and inequality, economic mobility, educational equity, and evidence-based public policy.

Teaching

Teaching economics through application.

I have served as an Instructor of Record and Teaching Assistant at UNC Charlotte, teaching and supporting courses across microeconomics, macroeconomics, econometrics, game theory, international business economics and finance, energy markets, and managerial economics.

As Instructor of Record, I designed and taught undergraduate Managerial Economics, using interactive lectures, case studies, assignments, examinations, and applied projects to connect economic theory with real-world decision-making.

Teaching interests: Microeconomics · Econometrics · Managerial Economics · Public Policy · Economic Development · Applied Economics
