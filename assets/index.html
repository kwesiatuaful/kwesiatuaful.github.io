ABOUT

Research with policy relevance

My research sits at the intersection of economics and public policy. I am particularly interested in understanding whether policies and institutions achieve their intended outcomes—and what happens when they do not.

My work examines economic development, natural-resource governance, institutions, poverty and inequality, and policy effectiveness. I use applied econometric and program-evaluation methods to study causal relationships and translate empirical evidence into policy-relevant insights.

RESEARCH

Selected research

My current research focuses on the relationship between natural resources, institutions, economic policy, and development outcomes.

DISSERTATION • DEVELOPMENT ECONOMICS

Natural Resource Wealth and Economic Development in Sub-Saharan Africa

A broader research agenda examining how natural-resource wealth, governance, and policy institutions influence economic growth and distributional outcomes.

Focus: Growth • Poverty • Inequality • Institutions

IMF • CAUSAL INFERENCE

IMF Managing Natural Resource Wealth – Thematic Fund

Examining whether IMF technical assistance in natural-resource management affects institutional performance, economic growth, poverty, and income inequality.

Methods: Staggered DiD • IV • SEM • Panel Econometrics

GOVERNANCE • SURVEY DATA

Citizen Perceptions of Natural Resource Governance

Studying how citizens perceive natural-resource governance and how those perceptions relate to policy effectiveness and development outcomes.

Data: Afrobarometer • Governance Indicators

EQUITY • POLICY EVALUATION

Economic Mobility and Educational Equity

Research on disparities in opportunity, educational outcomes, economic mobility, and the effectiveness of place-based and education policies.

Methods: DiD • RD • Synthetic Control • Matching
